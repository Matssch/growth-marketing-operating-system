# ICP Framework

An ICP should help the team say **no**.

If every company can fit the ICP, it is not an ICP.

## Fit dimensions

I would score accounts across six areas.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Company fit | Is this the type and size of business we serve well? |
| Problem fit | Do they have a problem FlowOps solves clearly? |
| Operational complexity | Is the problem important enough to justify change? |
| Commercial value | Is the potential contract meaningful? |
| Ability to buy | Can the company realistically purchase and implement? |
| Strategic value | Does winning this account create additional value? |

## Score

Each dimension receives a score from 1–5.

The score is not treated as objective truth.

It is a way to make assumptions visible and force prioritisation.

### Example

| Account | Fit | Problem | Complexity | Value | Ability | Strategic | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Northstar Logistics | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 27 |
| BrightDesk | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 20 |
| LocalWorks | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 13 |

The next step is to combine fit with actual behaviour.

A perfect-fit account showing no buying activity should not always receive the same treatment as a strong-fit account actively researching the category.
